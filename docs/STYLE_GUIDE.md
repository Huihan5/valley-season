# Valley Season — Writing Style Guide

This document is the reference for all narrative text in `src/data/`. Every scene description, dialogue line, event text, and ending script must be consistent with these standards.

---

## Tone

Controlled. Precise. A cold-palette beauty.

Reference authors: W.G. Sebald's architectural descriptions, Orhan Pamuk's Istanbul passages. The writing should make the reader feel temperature, hear echoes.

**Do:** Write with restraint. Let concrete sensory detail carry emotion. Trust the reader to infer.
**Don't:** Use fantasy clichés, purple prose, exclamation marks, or overt emotional narration. This world is ancient, rational, and built to last — not magical or wondrous.

---

## Sensory Palette — Autumn in Valewisp

### Visual
- Forest canopy: late September yellow-green → mid-October gold-red → late October deep brown
- Morning fog rises from the river, pooling in low ground as white "lakes"; rooftops emerge from the mist
- Afternoon sun cuts through thinning canopy, casting moving light-patches on the ground
- Dusk is long — the sky grades from amber in the west to indigo in the east

### Sound
- Wind through almost-falling leaves: a continuous dry rustle, crisper than summer
- Harvest rhythm: sickles cutting into wheat stalks in cadence
- Morning: crow flocks. Dusk: nesting birds returning to the woods
- Night: near-silence. Distant livestock. Wood cracking in the fireplace.

### Smell
- Core scent: leaf-mould — sweet, heavy, damp earth and decay
- Harvest adds: dry-cut wheat straw, sharp grass
- From the orchards: apple tartness
- Kitchen always: smoked meat and fresh bread
- After dark: air turns piercing clean, like ice-washed

### Touch
- Morning air: a film of cool on skin, warming quickly once the sun arrives
- Stone walls: sun-warmed by day, ice-cold by night
- Forest air: damp, with a weight that makes clothes faintly heavy

## Religion — Language Rules

Marigni's national religion is the Temple Forged by the Sacred Flame (圣火铸神殿). It is a craft-based institutional religion, NOT a pastoral/spiritual one.

### Terminology Map

| ❌ Don't use | ✅ Use instead | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Father, Pastor, Priest | Master, Forge-keeper, 匠师, 炉匠 | Clergy are craftspeople |
| Church, Chapel | Forge-chapel, 炉堂 | Worship spaces reference the forge |
| Prayer (in Christian sense) | Meditation, vigil, tending the flame | Practice is contemplative craft |
| Blessing | Tempering, annealing | Positive rites use metallurgical terms |
| Sin, redemption | Flaw, reforging | Moral language uses craft metaphors |
| Soul | Core, inner metal | The self as material to be worked |
| Scripture | The Codex of Fires, forge-texts | Written tradition |
| Congregation | The Hearth, those who tend | Community language |

### Lorenz's Voice
Master Lorenz is a Forge-keeper (炉匠) — the local religious figure. His speech is soft, unhurried, often uses questions to lead you to your own answer. His metaphors come from craft: mending, heating, cooling, tempering.

Example: "你觉得Hartmann为什么要走呢？有些东西在炉子里烧太久了就变脆了。但如果温度对，也许还能回火。"

NOT: "上帝自有安排" / "一切都会好的" / any pastoral comfort language.

## Forgehold vs Valewisp — Temperature Contrast

Characters from or influenced by Forgehold (铸都) speak and behave with **cold precision**: fewer words, exact phrasing, no emotional leakage. This is cultural, not personal — Forgehold is "凛冽的诗意."

Valewisp locals speak with **warm pragmatism**: more words, direct but not cold, rooted in seasons and soil. "急" is an insult here; things have their own timetable.

When these two temperatures meet in a scene (e.g., Kessler auditing the estate, or Von Alder's letters arriving), the contrast should be felt by the reader without being explained.

## Scene Description Structure

Each location description should follow this layered approach:

1. **One visual anchor** — the first thing the eye catches
2. **One non-visual sense** — sound, smell, or touch
3. **One detail that implies time or change** — something that tells you the season is moving

Example (Maple Avenue, mid-October):
> 枫径大道两侧的行道树正在燃烧——金红色的叶片在风中翻转，偶尔一片脱离枝头，慢悠悠地落在石板路面上，发出干燥的轻响。空气里有一股清冽的甜味，是落叶腐殖和远处果园混合在一起的味道。昨天这条路上还没有这么多落叶。

Three sentences. Visual → sound → smell → time.

## Things to Never Write

- "魔法" or anything implying supernatural magic (this world has none)
- Exclamation marks in narration (dialogue OK for Marta)
- The narrator expressing opinions or emotions ("令人惊叹的景色")
- Real-world brand names or anachronistic technology
- Any Christian religious terminology (see table above)
- Clichéd fantasy phrases ("古老的传说", "命运的齿轮", "神秘的力量")
